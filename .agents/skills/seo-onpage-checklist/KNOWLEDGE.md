# On-page and semantic SEO reference

Use `SKILL.md` for the review. Get the audience, site structure and copy conventions from the consuming project.

## 1. Search intent

Identify what the reader needs to learn or do. Compare the page with observed query results and available Search Console data. Record the query, market and date. Informational, navigational, commercial and transactional labels help organize the review. They do not replace evidence about this audience.

Check whether the page fulfils its purpose. Repeating keywords does not answer the reader's question. Do not require a page to be longer than current results or prescribe a word count.

## 2. Experience, expertise and trust

Google describes E-E-A-T as a way to think about content quality, not one specific ranking factor. Check accuracy, relevant experience or expertise and clear responsibility for the content. Use genuine evidence. Do not invent author histories or credentials.

For topics that could significantly affect health, financial stability, safety or society, assess whether the content needs qualified expert review. A personal experience does not automatically support professional advice. Evaluate the page's actual claims and potential harm, rather than labeling an entire industry from its name.

## 3. Coverage and overlapping pages

Check that readers can complete their task without missing essential information. Include related concepts when useful. Avoid keyword stuffing and mass-produced pages with little added value.

Map pages with similar purposes before recommending consolidation. Shared vocabulary alone does not prove that two pages compete or should be merged. Use page content and query evidence.

## 4. Headings

Make the main topic easy to identify. Organize sections with descriptive headings and logical semantic levels. Use styles for visual size. Treat the project's heading convention as an implementation choice, not a Google ranking formula.

## 5. Titles and descriptions

Use descriptive, concise titles that distinguish pages. Avoid boilerplate and keyword repetition. Google may generate a different title link from page headings or other signals.

Write relevant page descriptions. Google may use the description or choose text from the page for a particular query. Google specifies no fixed character limit for either field. Display depends on available width. CLI length warnings are review heuristics.

Follow the project's title separator convention. Do not infer a ranking gain from a delimiter or a particular keyword position.

## 6. Internal links

Use crawlable links and descriptive anchor text. Link relevant pages so readers and Google can discover them. Investigate orphaned pages before changing navigation. The checklist does not impose a universal click-depth limit.

## 7. Readability and structured data

Answer the reader's question clearly, then explain the evidence and next steps. Choose lists, tables or question-and-answer sections when they help. Do not attach a citation guarantee to a writing format.

For structured data, follow the applicable Google feature guide and match visible content. Google's AI features require no special markup. Verify the current feature documentation before proposing a rich result. Do not invent ratings or use fabricated reviews.

Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026 and removed the feature documentation on June 15. A useful visible FAQ can remain, but do not promise Google FAQ rich results or add FAQPage markup for that retired benefit. Existing valid markup need not be deleted solely because this Google feature ended. Review other consumers and dependencies.

## 8. Changes and follow-up

For search changes after an update, first check that the rollout has finished. Google recommends waiting at least a full week after completion before comparing performance. Inspect affected pages and search types. Avoid attributing a change to one cause without supporting evidence. Useful improvements may take time to appear in Search.

Review AI-generated content and metadata before publication. Producing many pages without added value can violate Google's scaled-content-abuse policy. The October 1, 2026 revision of Google's AI-content guidance points to rater criteria for low effort, originality and added value. Rater scores do not directly set rankings. Require a concrete contribution beyond a rephrased result set before expanding a template or a service/location matrix.

## 9. Content review by page type

Use [CONTENT_REVIEW.md](CONTENT_REVIEW.md) for the item-level record. Check instructions and limitations for guides, truthful date handling for blogs, experience evidence and comparisons for reviews, and actual offer details for landing and product pages. These are kit-defined review criteria, not a deterministic Google ranking formula. Missing tools or expertise leave the relevant item unmeasured. Common checks must not be replaced by metadata-only automation.

For Article/BlogPosting markup, assess applicable recommended properties and visible-content agreement. The current Google Article guide has no universally required properties. Do not invent a required author, date or headline rule for every page. A useful review provides evidence for claimed experience and explains relevant alternatives and trade-offs without fabricated tests.

## Conditional specialization evidence

Use the checklist's local, ecommerce, international and migration blocks only when relevant. Check actual operations and offers, useful product information, accessible language versions and retained page purpose. Do not invent locations, stock, reviews or policies. Do not prescribe automatic deletion for unavailable products.
